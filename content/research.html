Five candidates are competing for two council seats. Before comparing their promises, understand the projects and problems they would inherit. The council makes policy, budget and land-use decisions; a candidate’s chance of winning says nothing about whether they would do the job well.

1 · A proposed industrial redevelopment

What is the Quarry project?

Guadalupe Quarry is an existing quarry on San Bruno Mountain’s east face, southwest of Brisbane. Orchard Partners wants to close it and build the Quarry Innovation Center: about 895,000 square feet of light fabrication (making, assembling or packaging products), a parking garage and a 49.9-megawatt electricity substation. It would bring land into the city and protect or dedicate some open space.

Why it matters: council decisions would shape industrial activity, traffic, noise, habitat and infrastructure near town. The August 2026 notice explicitly excludes a data center. This is a proposal undergoing environmental review, not an approved project.

The choice to examine: what scale, safeguards and enforceable public benefits would each candidate require?

2 · A much larger housing and commercial plan

What are the Baylands?

The Baylands are a separate development area on former rail-yard and landfill land. Cleanup must address contamination before redevelopment. The plan under discussion would add 1,800–2,200 homes, up to 7 million square feet of commercial space and more than 100 acres of open space and amenities. It would substantially change the scale of Brisbane.

Why it matters: housing obligations, cleanup, water, transport and long-term city finances meet in one project. The city’s May 2026 plan release lists site cleanup, reliable water, revenue-positive operations and soil testing among its commitments.

The choice to examine: which conditions and delivery dates can be enforced, and who pays for infrastructure and cleanup? Supporting “responsible development” alone does not answer that.

3 · The city’s ability to manage its money

What went wrong with financial reporting?

Annual financial reports tell residents and lenders how public money was handled. Brisbane fell behind. The city says S&P withdrew its rating because audits were late, and auditors found material weaknesses and significant deficiencies in financial controls.

What is happening now: the city scheduled the FY2025 report for November 5 and reports staffing and procedure changes to clear the backlog. A scheduled report is not a restored credit rating.

Accountability: Davis served on council during these failures. It is fair to examine oversight; the reviewed records do not establish that she personally caused them. Challengers should also explain how they would prevent a repeat.

4 · Everyday services and public places

What would change in daily life?

The older bank property at 70 Old County Road, vacant storefronts, community gathering spaces and routine services are separate from the two major development proposals. Candidates suggest different uses, business support and accountability measures.

The choice to examine: is there a funded plan, a responsible operator, a delivery date and a way to measure success? A proposed café or community space is not yet a signed operator or an operating service.

The council unanimously approved a temporary pause on new data-center applications on October 1. Its first extension is scheduled for November 5, not yet adopted. This is not a permanent ban.

Fire and emergency coverage

Education: city influence has limits

Separate school districts govern schools. Council can affect childcare, safe routes, facilities coordination and land planning, but does not run classroom instruction. Davis’s stated school-site and childcare work should be assessed within that scope; the school-board contest remains a separate vote.

What is feasible at 70 Old County?

The city bought this gateway parcel in 2022. Its November 2025 consultant discussion found mixed-income/mixed-use housing viable, commercial use dependent on an anchor or broader redevelopment, and a standalone community center infeasible. These are feasibility findings, not a final construction plan; the project page’s timeline is stale.

What counts as a clear development benefit?

Baylands policy already requires safe remediation and a revenue-positive project. The development agreement is the contractual tool for benefits and obligations. General diligence questions include net revenue after service and lifecycle costs, delivery milestones, financial guarantees, impact limits, independent monitoring and enforceable remedies. These are review questions, not verified negotiated terms.

Housing compliance is a real constraint

HCD’s June 30 letter revoked compliance after the May 18 rezoning deadline was missed. It documents warnings dating to December 2023 and changing city timelines. Builder’s Remedy exposure is part of the actual decision environment; no later recertification was verified. The campaign explanation about late developer submissions does not, by itself, resolve the wider oversight history.
