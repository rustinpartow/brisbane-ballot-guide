Data centers: a moratorium is already in place

The council unanimously approved a temporary pause on new data-center applications on October 1. Its first extension is scheduled for November 5, not yet adopted. This is not a permanent ban.

City · Oct 1 moratorium / Oct 16 update ↗

The revised Quarry proposal is not a data center

The August revision proposes roughly 895,000 square feet of light fabrication and a 49.9 MW substation. The city expressly excludes a data center from this proposal. Environmental scoping is not project approval.

The accounting problem is documented

The city acknowledges overdue reports, rating withdrawals, and internal-control weaknesses. FY2025 audit consideration is scheduled November 5; staffing and procedural repairs are underway. No restored rating was verified. Collective oversight is relevant; individual causation is not established.

What the city can actually deliver.

Separate the choice of a representative from the operating facts and the tools available to City Hall.

Police, public disorder & homelessness response

The official blotter is selective and expressly cannot establish year-to-year crime trends. This review did not verify a current comparable crime series or comprehensive operating proposal from each candidate. Measure response times, repeat calls, staffing, complaint resolution and county service coordination rather than treating anecdotes as a trend.

Fire and emergency coverage

NCFA’s 2025 report gives Brisbane an average emergency response time of 5:30. That is an agency performance measure, not an individual candidate’s accomplishment; an average also does not show slowest responses or future wildfire readiness.

NCFA · 2025 annual performance report ↗

Education: city influence has limits

Separate school districts govern schools. Council can affect childcare, safe routes, facilities coordination and land planning, but does not run classroom instruction. Davis’s stated school-site and childcare work should be assessed within that scope; the school-board contest remains a separate vote.

Projects: promises need terms.

A café, a public space and a major development each need a workable delivery plan.

Can a better café actually open?

No reviewed candidate material establishes a signed new coffee operator. A workable recruitment plan needs a suitable space, tenant economics, permitting, fit-out funding, customer demand and a delivery date. Beautification can support this but does not itself secure a business.

What is feasible at 70 Old County?

The city bought this gateway parcel in 2022. Its November 2025 consultant discussion found mixed-income/mixed-use housing viable, commercial use dependent on an anchor or broader redevelopment, and a standalone community center infeasible. These are feasibility findings, not a final construction plan; the project page’s timeline is stale.

City · 70 Old County Road project ↗City · dated council meeting highlights ↗

What counts as a clear development benefit?

Baylands policy already requires safe remediation and a revenue-positive project. The development agreement is the contractual tool for benefits and obligations. General diligence questions include net revenue after service and lifecycle costs, delivery milestones, financial guarantees, impact limits, independent monitoring and enforceable remedies. These are review questions, not verified negotiated terms.

City · Baylands requirements and agreements ↗

Housing compliance is a real constraint

HCD’s June 30 letter revoked compliance after the May 18 rezoning deadline was missed. It documents warnings dating to December 2023 and changing city timelines. Builder’s Remedy exposure is part of the actual decision environment; no later recertification was verified. The campaign explanation about late developer submissions does not, by itself, resolve the wider oversight history.
