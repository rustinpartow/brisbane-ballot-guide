Read the fine print, comfortably

The forecast, explained.

All win probabilities are the analyst’s estimates. Sources supply election results, polls, candidate statements and ballot text; they do not endorse these probabilities. No past forecast calibration was performed.

  • Poll-informed: Governor and insurance commissioner incorporate verified October polls, with judgmental allowance for polling error and undecided voters.
  • Prior-driven: Other races use incumbency, previous results, campaign-reported endorsements or a broad partisan baseline. These are weaker estimates.
  • Multiple winners: A council candidate’s number is the chance of taking either seat. Probabilities sum to 200% for two seats and 300% for three seats, not 100%.
  • Judicial retention: The same 99% retention prior is applied to each justice. Individual decisions, conduct and opposition campaigns were not comprehensively reviewed.
  • Sensitivity bands: The ranges shown are subjective plausible probability ranges under changed assumptions. They are not statistical confidence intervals.

Evidence about one challenger is uneven. A thinner reviewed record is not evidence of a weaker candidate. Fundraising filings, a full candidate-forum review or a credible new poll could materially change local estimates.

Before you submit

Make it count.

Brisbane City Hall vote center: October 31–November 2, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.; November 3, 7 a.m.–8 p.m.

Return in person to an official vote center or drop box by 8 p.m. November 3. Mail ballots must be postmarked by November 3 and received by November 10. Close to Election Day, request a hand postmark at a post-office counter or use an official drop box.

Check the exact ballot. This guide covers the verified city, state, federal, county and judicial contests plus potential school-district items. It has not been matched to a residential address; additional school or special-district contests may apply. District-dependent rows are marked.
